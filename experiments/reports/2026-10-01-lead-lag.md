# Binance → Aster/Lighter lead/lag on the September 27 capture

## Question

At our collector, when the Binance BTC-perp mid moves by at least X bps, does
the Aster or Lighter mid follow? If it does, does a taker round trip on the lag
venue beat two taker fees? The round trip enters at t0 + L and exits at
t0 + L + h, matching the time-exit policy chosen for a future
`LeadLagStrategy`.

This report gives conditional statistics, **not simulated PnL**. It models no
queue position, partial fills, inventory limits or overlapping positions.

## Method

- Dataset `6b556515-…`, one complete Capture Run. Train is Capture Sequence
  `[1, 2,403,822)` (about 13.8 h) and validation is `[2,403,822, 3,514,231)`
  (about 4.6 h). The **test split was not loaded.**
- Code: `experiments/analysis/lead_lag.py` (unit-tested in
  `experiments/tests/test_lead_lag.py`) and
  `experiments/notebooks/07_lead_lag.ipynb`.
- Book state at time t is the last accepted top-of-book snapshot at or before
  t, on Local Receive Time. An Order Book outage removes the state until the
  next snapshot, and a quote older than 1 s is unusable.
- **Event:** the Binance mid changes by ≥ X bps against its value 200 ms
  earlier. Two Binance snapshots fall inside that window, since Binance
  updates about every 100 ms. A 2 s cooldown keeps event windows from
  overlapping.
- **Round trip:** after an up move, buy the lag ask at t0 + L and sell the lag
  bid at t0 + L + h; after a down move, do the reverse. `gross` includes
  crossing the spread twice, and `net = gross − 2 × fee`. Binance is never
  traded (ADR 0010).
- **Selection rule, fixed before looking at validation:** for each venue, take
  the (X, h) cell with the best train mean net edge at a 5 bps fee and
  L = 50 ms, among cells with at least 10 events.

## Data facts that bound the result

| Venue | Update p50 | Update p99 | Spread p50 | Spread p90 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Binance | 102 ms | 336 ms | 0.012 bps | 0.012 bps |
| Aster | 107 ms | 196 ms | 0.012 bps | 0.012 bps |
| Lighter | 51 ms | 253 ms | 0.012 bps | 0.56 bps |

These figures are from train; validation is similar.

- All three books usually sit at a one-tick spread (about $0.10 at about
  $84k). The spread costs almost nothing, so fees decide the outcome.
- **Lead resolution is about 100 ms**, set by the Binance partial-depth
  cadence. A lead shorter than one Binance interval can't be seen in books.

## Results

### The lead is real

Events per hour: X = 2 bps gives 17 in train and 41 in validation; X = 5 bps
gives 0.8 in train and 3.5 in validation; X = 10 bps gives 1 and 2 events in
total. Validation was more volatile.

After a Binance move, the lag mid follows in the same direction. The table
shows the median capture ratio (lag move / Binance move) on train:

| Venue | X | 100 ms | 250 ms | 500 ms | 1 s | 2 s |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Aster | 2 | 0.23 | 0.70 | 0.88 | 0.94 | 0.97 |
| Aster | 5 | 0.41 | 0.71 | 0.84 | 0.91 | 0.90 |
| Lighter | 2 | 0.02 | 0.60 | 0.90 | 1.02 | 1.06 |
| Lighter | 5 | 0.00 | 0.19 | 0.64 | 0.93 | 0.86 |

**Catch-up, not shared drift.** The capture ratio compares the lag move after
t0 with the Binance move before t0. If Binance kept moving after t0, part of
the "response" would just be the whole market drifting. So Binance's own
signed move after t0 was measured the same way. Mean moves in bps, train
(validation within about 0.5 bps):

| X | Venue | h | Binance before t0 | Binance after t0 | Lag after t0 | Catch-up (lag − Binance after) |
| ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2 | Aster | 1 s | 2.65 | +0.34 | 2.55 | 2.21 |
| 2 | Lighter | 1 s | 2.65 | +0.34 | 2.69 | 2.35 |
| 2 | Lighter | 2 s | 2.65 | +0.44 | 2.79 | 2.35 |
| 5 | Aster | 1 s | 6.60 | −0.96 | 5.32 | 6.28 |
| 5 | Lighter | 1 s | 6.60 | −0.96 | 5.91 | 6.87 |
| 5 | Lighter | 2 s | 6.60 | −0.08 | 5.83 | 5.92 |

- At X = 2 bps, Binance keeps drifting by +0.3 to +0.45 bps. That is about
  13–17% of the lag move at 1–2 s: shared momentum, not lead/lag. The rest is
  catch-up.
- At X = 5 bps, Binance partly *reverts* after t0, while the lag venues still
  move +5 to +6 bps toward Binance's earlier level. That is pure catch-up.
- Lighter's capture ratio above 1 at X = 2 (1.02 at 1 s, 1.06 at 2 s) is
  explained by that small Binance drift.

**Timing:**
- The first lag mid change comes a median of about 40–85 ms after the
  triggering Binance snapshot (X = 2 and 5; the X = 10 cells have n ≤ 2). It
  moves in the Binance direction in 88–100% of events.
- These delays are about half of each venue's update interval (Aster
  ~107 ms, Lighter ~51 ms). They mostly measure when the next snapshot
  happens to arrive, not how fast the venue reacts. The capture-ratio curve
  is the meaningful timing measure.
- Aster reacts faster. Lighter takes about 0.5–1 s to absorb the move.
- Cross-correlation of mid returns, on 50 ms bars (primary) and 100 ms bars
  (cross-check), peaks at Binance leading Aster by about 100 ms in both
  splits.
- Lighter's correlation curve is flat across 0–300 ms: it absorbs moves
  gradually, not after one fixed delay. Its peak sits at 200–250 ms on train
  and 0 ms on validation, so the peak position isn't a stable lag estimate.
- Why 50 ms bars: a sweep from 10 to 500 ms shows that bars shorter than the
  ~100 ms Binance cadence add no resolution (99.8% of 10 ms returns are zero,
  peak correlation 0.06). Bars of 200 ms or more push the lead into k = 0.

### But the gross edge doesn't cover a 5 bps taker fee

Gross edge in bps at L = 50 ms (mean / median; n = number of events):

| Venue | X | h | Train n | Train gross | Validation n | Validation gross |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Aster | 2 | 1 s | 240 | 2.12 / 2.04 | 188 | 1.86 / 1.65 |
| Aster | 5 | 2 s | 11 | 4.29 / 3.13 | 16 | 3.71 / 4.18 |
| Lighter | 2 | 1 s | 240 | 2.35 / 2.23 | 188 | 1.89 / 1.83 |
| Lighter | 5 | 2 s | 11 | 5.40 / 5.03 | 16 | 4.89 / 5.45 |

- The selection rule picked **X = 5 bps, h = 2 s for both venues**. Here is
  that cell's mean net edge by fee:

| Venue | Split | Fee 0 | Fee 2.5 bps | Fee 5 bps |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Aster | train | +4.29 | −0.71 | −5.71 |
| Aster | validation | +3.71 | −1.29 | −6.29 |
| Lighter | train | +5.40 | +0.40 | −4.60 |
| Lighter | validation | +4.89 | −0.11 | −5.11 |

- **Break-even taker fee per leg ≈ gross / 2:** in this cell, 1.86 bps
  (validation) to 2.15 bps (train) for Aster, and 2.45–2.70 bps for Lighter.
  For X = 2 bps it is about 0.9–1.2 bps.
- **Latency:** at X = 5 and h = 2 s, gross edge falls from 5.4 to 3.2 bps
  (train) and from 4.6 to 2.6 bps (validation) as Aster's L goes from 0 to
  100 ms. Lighter loses only about 0.5–0.7 bps over the same range, because
  it absorbs the move slowly.
- **h = 2 s is the largest horizon tested**, and the edge is still slightly
  rising there. The real optimum may be longer, but a longer hold also adds
  directional risk unrelated to the lead.

### At the account tiers in use

Checked against the venue docs on 2026-10-01:

| Venue and account | Maker | Taker | Venue taker delay | Taker round-trip cost |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Aster base tier | 0 | 4 bps | — | 8 bps |
| Lighter Standard | 0 | 0 | 300 ms (cancels too; makers 0 ms) | 0 |

Each venue is evaluated at its own cost: Aster at fee 4 bps and L = 50 ms;
Lighter at fee 0 and L = 350 ms (300 ms delay + 50 ms assumed network). These
cells come from the docs, not from a grid search. Mean net edge in bps, train
/ validation (hit rate in brackets):

| Venue | X | h = 500 ms | h = 1 s | h = 2 s |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Aster | 2 | −6.11 / −6.32 | −5.88 / −6.14 | −5.83 / −6.01 |
| Aster | 5 | −4.39 / −4.93 | −4.05 / −4.78 | −3.71 / −4.29 |
| Lighter | 2 | +0.25 / +0.02 | +0.36 / +0.15 | +0.41 / +0.21 (62% / 48%) |
| Lighter | 5 | +0.86 / +0.12 | +1.15 / +0.57 | +1.47 / +0.82 (82% / 63%) |

- **Aster, taker in and out: not viable.** It loses about 4–6 bps per trade
  in every cell, because the 8 bps round-trip cost is about twice the gross
  edge.
- **Lighter, taker in and out: positive but thin.** The 300 ms delay removes
  most of the edge: at X = 5 and h = 2 s it falls from +5.4 bps at L = 50 ms
  to +1.5 bps (train) and +0.8 bps (validation).
  - At X = 2 bps, the edge is +0.2–0.4 bps with a hit rate near 50%. That is
    within reach of any small unmodelled cost, such as depth beyond the best
    level or slippage.
  - At X = 5 bps, the edge is +0.8–1.5 bps, but over only 11–14 events, at
    roughly 1–3 events per hour.
- **Maker orders are where the edge could be.** Both venues charge 0% maker,
  and Lighter makers aren't delayed. An Aster maker exit halves the cost to
  4 bps, about break-even against the gross edge. A Lighter maker entry avoids
  the delay. Neither is measured yet; see the plan below.

The simulator's defaults now use these tiers: `taker_fee_bps` and
`taker_speed_bump_ns` in `simulator/config.py`.

## Conclusion

1. **Binance leads Aster and Lighter at our collector.** The lag venues absorb
   roughly 85–100% of a Binance move within 0.5–1 s, with Aster faster. This
   is catch-up rather than shared drift: Binance's own move after the trigger
   is small (+0.3 to +0.45 bps at X = 2) or slightly reverting (X = 5).
2. **A taker-in, taker-out round trip does not beat a 5 bps fee per leg** in
   either venue, split or cell. The signal is only worth trading if the real
   all-in cost per leg is below about 2–2.5 bps. That means a low-fee tier, a
   maker entry or exit, or both.
   - At our actual tiers, Aster taker/taker loses 4–6 bps per trade.
   - Lighter taker/taker keeps +0.2–1.5 bps after its 300 ms taker delay.
     That is thin and rests on few events.
3. The event count at X ≥ 5 bps is small: 11 train and 16 validation events.
   The validation results agree with train in sign and size, but they aren't a
   robust estimate.

## Proposed next measurement: maker execution

**Goal:** find out whether a resting order keeps the edge that taker orders
lose to fees (Aster) and to the speed bump (Lighter). Two variants:

1. **Taker entry, maker exit.** After entering, rest an exit limit order at
   the lag venue's touch on the profitable side (sell at the ask after a buy).
   If it isn't filled by h_max, fall back to a taker exit.
   - Aster cost: 4 bps if the maker exit fills, 8 bps if it falls back.
   - Lighter: the exit avoids the 300 ms delay, but the entry still pays it.
2. **Maker entry, by repricing a resting quote.** On a Binance move, place or
   move a passive quote on the lag venue toward the Binance level, and capture
   the catch-up when lag-venue takers hit it.
   - Lighter makers have no delay, so this side-steps the speed bump.
   - This is close to the planned second strategy, market making with an
     external fair value.

**Fill model.** Today the simulator treats a limit order as taker-like:
- it fills against book levels whenever the book crosses;
- it pays the taker fee.

Cancel latency is implemented: a cancel takes effect after the order latency
plus `cancel_speed_bump_ns` (300 ms on Lighter Standard), and the target can
still fill until then.

The fill model needs these decisions, owned by the human (AGENTS.md §2,
execution assumptions):

- **Fill trigger:** does a resting order fill when a lag-venue trade prints
  at or through its price, or only when the price trades *through* it
  (conservative, so no queue position is needed)?
- **Queue position:** at the back of the visible quantity at that price,
  with fills consumed by later trades at that price; or ignored, with fills
  only on a trade through.
- **Fill price and fee:** the limit price, maker fee (0 on both venues), and
  no slippage.
- **Cancel latency:** done, with order latency plus 300 ms on Lighter
  Standard. Fills can still land while a cancel is in flight.
- **Adverse selection** has to be reported, not assumed away. For each fill,
  report the lag-venue mid move after the fill. A maker that only gets filled
  just before the price moves against it isn't capturing the lead.
- **Data limit:** Aster trades are recorded as aggregates. A queue model on
  Aster can't see individual fills inside an aggregate.

**Output:** the same tables as section 4, by variant, venue, X and h:
- fill rate;
- time to fill;
- net edge per filled round trip and per signal, including the cost of
  fallbacks;
- adverse selection.

Train and validation only.

**Build order:**
1. Add a maker/taker flag to fills and `maker_fee_bps` to the simulator,

   already done.
2. Write a research `LeadLagStrategy` with time exit at h, for taker/taker

3. Run variant 2 only if variant 1 shows that maker fills survive adverse
   selection.

## Caveats

- One capture. More days are needed, across calm and volatile conditions.
- The lead is measured at our VPS and includes each feed's path. It is not
  exchange-level causality.
- No exchange-time diagnostic is shown. Exchange times are in the separate
  `exchange_times` table, joined on `capture_sequence`. It holds book event
  times for all three venues. The diagnostic that separates venue-side
  timing from the feed path hasn't been run yet.
- L is an assumption, not measured order latency. The 300 ms Lighter delay
  comes from the venue docs. The 50 ms network part isn't measured.
- Fee tiers change; they were checked on 2026-10-01. The Aster table assumes
  BTC is in the "General" fee group, not "Group B" (10 bps taker).
- Events are taken from 100 ms Binance snapshots. A trade-based trigger was
  tested separately; `/market` aggregate trades are sometimes held back by
  about 150 ms on Binance's side, so they are not a clean faster signal (see
  the book-quality report).
- **Binance book quality: resolved, see `2026-10-01-binance-book-quality.md`.**
  The depth10 book is an exact 100 ms sample of the real-time book. The
  "trades outside the book" figure came from joining each trade to a book
  that already showed the same match. Book-based event times are late by
  about 40 ms on average (at most about 100 ms) compared with `bookTicker`.
  Detecting earlier is worth about +0.1 (unconditional) to +0.4 (matched,
  optimistic) bps gross at X = 2, which doesn't change the conclusions above.
- Quantity isn't modelled: top-of-book depth and market impact are ignored.
